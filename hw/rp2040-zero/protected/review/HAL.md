## Source and recovery disposition

- **U1 only.** IC1 supplies A5. IC9 supplies SYS5, IC4 supplies U3 and IC8/IC6 supply U4 from A. With J2 cabled but VBUS off, IC2 is the VBUS reverse barrier and IC11/IC10 must isolate J2 D±, even if U1 keeps the module in BOOTSEL. The ROM overrides VBUS detection. The hardware gate works only with the original receptacle removed and its SYS5-connected pads insulated. IC10 is parked on open D1 when J2_PRESENT is low. Clarify the phrase “opens native data” in the off case in CURRENT-FAULT.md to mean J2 D± are isolated. [Waveshare schematic](https://files.waveshare.com/upload/4/4c/RP2040_Zero.pdf) · [RP2040 ROM](https://github.com/raspberrypi/pico-bootrom-rp2040/blob/ef22cd8ede5bc007f81d7f2416b48db90f313434/usb_device_tiny/usb_device.c#L1313-L1320)
- **U2 only, both on, and A loss.** IC9 can feed SYS5 from B5 with GP8 low. U3 remains without a B feed. IC7 alone admits B_U4 to IC8, independently of IC9. The two muxes prefer qualified A separately and can transfer at different times. A loss can shift the entire local load to J2 with no firmware authorization, including while USB stays suspended. With an admitted B standby branch, aggregate J2 load also has to cover transferred U4 load and its transient. Required evidence is total J2 current and actual link state through cold default, reset, BOOTSEL, steady suspend and A loss while suspended, using the exact fitted load and named source. USB 2.0 suspend permits 2.5 mA. A 100 mA descriptor is not a forwarding grant. IC2 OVLO can leave B5 above IC8/IC9's 5.5 V recommended ceiling before cutoff. An identified source envelope and inrush/overshoot evidence are still required. [TI TPS2116](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2116.pdf) · [USB-IF electrical updates](https://compliance.usb.org/index.asp?UpdateFile=Electrical)
- **Raw J2 loss versus protected B5 loss.** IC11 J2_PRESENT asynchronously clears IC14 when sensed raw U2 falls. With GP8 stale high, restoration of raw voltage cannot generate a new clock edge. IC15 still denies when GP8 drops. B5 can collapse behind IC2 while U2_RAW and J2_PRESENT remain high, so the old Q survives with A powering 3V3_AON. IC7 can reset through its input power cycle and then energize with that old enable. A VBUS-held USB reset or suspend likewise supplies no hardware clear. Capture U2_RAW, B5, 3V3_AON, GP8, Q and IC7 EN through one raw-valid B5 interruption/recovery, a true raw-loss pulse and a VBUS-held reset/suspend. Establish current-owner retention under a valid aggregate budget or that the branch remains denied until fresh admission. [TI TLV3012B](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tlv3011.pdf) · [TI SN74LVC1G175](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lvc1g175.pdf) · [TI TPS2553](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2553.pdf)

## J4 forced drive and fault coordination

- **Both sources off.** IC6 is the first J4 reverse barrier and IC12 specifies powered-off high impedance at its stated supply conditions. With retained charge or a partly powered 3V3_AON, that endpoint condition does not cover sequencing. **A only** keeps AON alive and normally keeps HOST5 live, even in module reset. **B only denied** deliberately exposes the IC6-unpowered/AON-powered condition. **B only admitted** supplies HOST5 through IC7. **Both on** normally select A but can transfer to B under A droop. Across these cases, an external J4 supply near HOST5 can keep PGTH high before the reverse threshold is crossed. TI Table 7-5 gives PG low after tPGD when the reverse comparator qualifies. The up-to-1 V externally pulled PG clamp is a specified unpowered condition, not a ramp guarantee. IC13's screened 1.198–1.286 V trip clears that clamp under those conditions. PGTH's 4.080–4.255 V falling screen is also below the 4.75 V loaded-host criterion. Capture J4 VBUS, HOST5, AON, PG, J4_PATH_OK, D± and both raw-port currents during forced J4 from near equal to overdrive, HOST5 UVLO/ramp, source transfer and hard short. Include floating and held-off upstream inputs. [TI TPS25947 Table 7-5](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps25947.pdf) · [TI USB switch](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tmuxhs221f.pdf) · [USB-IF drop and droop method](https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/USB20_32_BC12_Drop_Droop_1_4_1.pdf)
- **Shorts and containment.** IC1/IC2 cannot interrupt a short on the connector side of their input. Those cases depend on the exact adapter, laptop port and cable plus carrier reverse isolation. A J3 short is local to IC4/A5 by intent. A J4 short draws through IC8/IC6 from A or through IC2/IC7/IC8/IC6 from B, with possible reassignment after A droop if B was admitted. TI distinguishes circuit-breaker response and latch-off after persistent overload from fast-trip followed by a current-limited recovery attempt for a hard short. PG can remain high through transient overcurrent subject to PGTH. The current thresholds alone do not establish trip order, fault energy or isolation of other loads. Recalculated Eq. 5 nominal IC1/IC2/IC4/IC6 thresholds of 1.249/0.507/0.507/0.622 A and J4 capacitor energy of 2.34–5.51 mJ at 5.25 V. These exclude fault-source delivery. On B, 200 + 37.3 + 2 mA exceeds the 220.8 mA screened IC7 minimum. The illustrative 175 mA case leaves 6.5 mA in that same screen and does not qualify startup or USB authorization. Check cold and running fault, clipping or latch outcome and whether A loss re-energizes an existing fault from B. Keep the retained D10 and connector-side D101/D121 protections in the assessed build. D10 vendor, cable and adapter protection still require a fit definition.
